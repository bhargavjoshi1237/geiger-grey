# Discovery, search and analytics interfaces over a knowledge base (state of market, October 2026)

Scope note: research ran 2026-10-21. The session's shared web-search budget ran out near the end, so a few items (Viable's status, Amplitude AI Feedback details, LightRAG WebUI, official Typesense/InfraNodus pricing) could not be checked and are listed under Gaps. Many prices come from third-party aggregators. Each is marked as such, and conflicts are shown side by side.

Status legend used throughout: **[ACQUIRED]**, **[RETIRED]**, **[WINDING DOWN]**, **[PIVOTED]**, **[MAINTENANCE MODE]**.
LLM-cost legend: **NO-LLM** = runs on stored embeddings, graph data, metadata or counts only, so it is cheap under bring-your-own (BYO) models. **EMB** = needs one embedding call per query or item (cheap). **LLM** = needs generative calls (the user's BYO key pays).

## 1. Semantic, hybrid and faceted search as a product surface: do users still want a search box rather than chat in 2026?

### Takeaway
Search has not gone away. The market has converged on **one box with two modes**: as-you-type keyword/hybrid results with facets, plus an "Ask AI" switch that turns the query into a cited answer (Algolia DocSearch v4, Kapa, Inkeep, Glean, Vertex AI Search). User research shows people pick by task. Chat wins for exploratory, unfamiliar or multi-constraint questions. A results list wins for verification, exact facts, high-stakes lookups and anything where users want control over sources. A results page with facets and the search analytics behind it (zero-result queries) is LLM-free apart from one query embedding. It is the cheapest high-value surface Grey could add.

### Cited Findings
**User preference evidence (search vs chat)**
- NN/g observational study, published 27 Feb 2026 (about 9 participants, real self-chosen tasks). People chose AI chat to start in an unfamiliar space (no keywords needed), to juggle several constraints, to cut clicks by aggregating across sites, and to ask for comparison tables. They chose traditional search to validate AI answers, to confirm prices and key facts ("I wouldn't trust it wholesale"), for high-stakes decisions, because of control ("I don't control the type of information or sources"), and to go to sources directly ("I like to look at the source directly"). Some found it hard to tell which source backed which claim in AI answers — [NN/g, "AI search and information seeking"](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-search-infoseeking/)
- Orbit Media survey (2026): 55% use AI chat as a primary or frequent research tool, but the share who *usually* use chat for research fell from 35% to 28%. Only 24% preferred AI chat for local/business queries. About 40% preferred chat for step-by-step instructions in both years — [Orbit Media](https://www.orbitmedia.com/blog/ai-vs-google/)
- Datos figures (cited secondhand) put AI tools at 1.83% of US desktop activity in Q2 2026 against 10.52% for traditional search — [Blazehive](https://www.blazehive.io/statistics/ai-search-statistics/) (secondary source; methodology unclear)
- 2023 arXiv comparison: participants rated ChatGPT's information quality higher than Google's at a similar level of trust — [arXiv 2307.01135](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.01135). A 2025 paper found older adults relied more on chat despite less familiarity with it — [InfoDocket summary](https://www.infodocket.com/2025/11/23/research-paper-toward-integrating-ai-chat-and-search-a-user-centered-perspective-across-age-groups/)
- Google made AI Mode the default at I/O 2026, so the search box now behaves like a chat thread — [RampIQ recap](https://rampiq.agency/blog/google-search-box-ai-chat-box/) (agency source). HN commenters objected that Google "just force[s] the user into a chat straight up" — [HN thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48197370)
- HN sentiment on chat-only portals: commenters said static documents plus decent search would serve a government services site better than an LLM chat box — [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49893509). Older thread: "AI chatbots are not a replacement for search engines" — [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=34131553)

**Products shipping search surfaces (UI/API shape, technique, pricing)**
- **Algolia DocSearch v4 (Sep 23, 2025):** users "switch instantly between as-you-type keyword search and Ask AI". Results flow into a cited AI answer with follow-ups. Algolia says developers "are expecting intuitive, smooth integration between search with citations and conversational AI". The post gives no usage split between the two modes — [Algolia blog](https://www.algolia.com/blog/product/docsearch-reimagined)
- **Algolia Ask AI:** BYO LLM ("connect your favorite LLM from your provider of choice"), grounded in the Algolia index, scoped with `facetFilters`/`filters`/`restrictSearchableAttributes`. **Now part of Agent Studio and not available standalone for new applications** — [Algolia Ask AI docs](https://www.algolia.com/doc/guides/algolia-ai/askai.md). DocSearch itself is free for technical docs — [Algolia Ask AI page](https://www.algolia.com/products/ai/ask-ai)
- **Algolia NeuralSearch (vector + keyword hybrid):** not on self-serve tiers. Third-party sources place it in the custom-contract Elevate plan. The free Build plan gives 10K requests/mo and 1M records. - **Algolia search analytics (NO-LLM):** a No Results Rate chart, a "Searches without Results" tab, and an API returning "the 1,000 most frequent searches that produced zero results" over a default 8-day window — [Algolia metrics](https://www.algolia.com/doc/guides/search-analytics/concepts/metrics); [no-results API](https://www.algolia.com/doc/rest-api/analytics/get-searches-no-results.md); [no-results-rate API](https://www.algolia.com/doc/rest-api/analytics/get-no-results-rate.md)

### Inferences
- **Pattern A — hybrid search results page** (BM25 + vector + reciprocal-rank fusion, as-you-type, snippets with highlighted passages). Technique: EMB (one query embedding through the user's BYO embedder; can fall back to pure BM25 with no model at all). Grey already has chunks and embeddings and exposes `/retrieve`. It needs: a full-text index (e.g. Postgres `tsvector`/BM25) next to vectors, a results UI that groups chunks by source document, highlighting, and pagination. Products show users expect both modes in one box. A good UX is a "Search / Ask" toggle in the Playground and in the hosted widget.
- **Pattern B — faceted filtering by source, type, date and entity.** NO-LLM at query time, because entity facets reuse knowledge-graph extraction that ingestion already paid for. Glean's `from`/`type` filters are the closest analogue. Grey needs: facet counts (aggregations over chunk metadata plus a chunk-to-entity join), a date-range facet, and a media-type facet (doc/audio/video/image/web/note). An "entity facet" is a differentiator, because most search vendors do not have a knowledge graph underneath.
- **Pattern C — search analytics** (top queries, zero-result queries, low-score queries, click-through). NO-LLM. Algolia and Document360 treat this as a core feature. Grey can compute a "no good match" signal from retrieval scores (top-1 similarity below a threshold) on every `/retrieve`, chat or MCP call without an LLM. This feeds straight into knowledge-gap analytics (Section 5).
- **Pattern D — query autocomplete and suggestions** from popular past queries and entity names. NO-LLM (prefix index over the query log and entity names).
- Demand signal: every major docs-AI vendor (Algolia, Kapa, Inkeep) ships search plus Ask AI rather than chat alone, and NN/g shows verification behaviour depends on seeing sources. A results list is the natural trust layer for Grey's chat.

### Gaps
- No vendor publishes a usage split between keyword search and Ask AI in the same box (Algolia's post has none). No reliable Glean statistic on search vs assistant query share was found.
- NN/g's sample size is inferred ("6 out of 9"). The 2025 arXiv paper "Blending Queries and Conversations" (arXiv 2504.05156) was surfaced but not read.
- Official Typesense Cloud and Algolia NeuralSearch prices were not verified on the vendor pages.

## 2. Similarity and recommendations: "more like this", related documents, next-best content, duplicate and near-duplicate detection, plagiarism and overlap checks

### Takeaway
Similarity features are the cheapest discovery surface over an existing vector store. "Related documents", "more like this" and near-duplicate detection need **no LLM calls at all**: they reuse ingestion-time embeddings or lexical hashes. Commercial proof is strongest in e-commerce recommendations (Algolia Recommend) and in knowledge-base hygiene (Guru Duplicate Detection, Intercom Fin's weekly duplicate and contradiction suggestions). Plagiarism checking is a separate, crowded consumer and education market.

### Cited Findings
- **Elasticsearch `more_like_this` is lexical:** it takes the source document's fields, picks the top-k TF-IDF terms and runs them as a disjunctive query. An academic search system uses it for related-document recommendations — [arXiv 2607.26380](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.26380). Term vectors help but are not required — [OpenAleph MLT docs](https://openaleph.org/docs/lib/openaleph-search/more_like_this/)
- **Elastic kNN** lists "Recommendations: product suggestions, collaborative filtering, content discovery" among its main use cases — [Elastic kNN docs](https://www.elastic.co/docs/solutions/search/vector/knn)
- **Algolia Recommend:** "Related items" combines click/conversion events with item attributes. "Frequently bought together" uses collaborative filtering in a relaxed variant and actual co-purchases in a strict variant. A "Looking Similar" display exists in the Shopify app — [Algolia Recommend guide](https://www.algolia.com/doc/guides/algolia-recommend); [Algolia Shopify Recommend](https://www.algolia.com/doc/integration/shopify/building-search-ui/recommend). Each objectID in a request counts as one billable request — [Algolia old Recommend docs](https://www.algolia.com/old-docs/deprecated/recommend/api-reference/recommend-react/useRecommendations/). A third party lists about $0.60 per 1K requests with a 14-day trial — [Adtribute](https://www.adtribute.io/directory/algolia-recommend)
- **Guru Duplicate Detection:** shows duplicate suggestions only to users with edit access. Merge and delete stay within assigned roles. Flagged cards keep their verification status — [Guru Duplicate Detection](https://getguru.com/features/duplicate-detection)
- **Intercom Fin content recommendations (Pro add-on):** weekly batches of up to 20 suggestions, typically about 15 potential contradictions and about 5 duplicates. Suggestions are generated by comparing failed Fin replies with successful human replies. Pending reviews expire after 4 weeks — [Intercom help](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/11394959-use-ai-powered-content-suggestions-to-improve-fin)
- **Near-duplicate technique stack:** exact dedup by content hash, near-dedup by MinHash/SimHash, and semantic dedup by embeddings; LSH buckets candidates — [ZeroEntropy dedup concepts](https://www.zeroentropy.dev/concepts/deduplication/). NVIDIA NeMo Curator ships exact (MD5), fuzzy (MinHash + LSH) and semantic (embedding) dedup — [NeMo Curator docs](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo/curator/25.09/curate-text/process-data/deduplication/index.html). Zilliz Cloud has a built-in MinHash function — [Zilliz](https://docs.zilliz.com/docs/minhash-function). ClickHouse ships `wordShingleMinHash()` and `wordShingleSimHash()` — [OneUptime guide](https://oneuptime.com/blog/post/2026-03-31-clickhouse-wordshingle-minhash/markdown). Embedding-based dedup catches same-meaning documents that are not textually similar, but is called "more experimental" at very large scale — [ZeroEntropy](https://www.zeroentropy.dev/concepts/deduplication/)
- **Plagiarism and overlap:** Copyleaks has an API, covers 100+ languages and flags paraphrased, translated and source-code plagiarism. Consumer prices conflict: $7.99/mo — [Dataconomy](https://dataconomy.com/tools/copyleaks/) — vs $16.99/mo — [Bestwriting](https://bestwriting.com/best-plagiarism-checker). Turnitin is sold only through institutional licences — [Bestwriting](https://bestwriting.com/best-plagiarism-checker); [CreativePool](https://creativepool.com/partner-articles/news/top-10-plagiarism-checkers-2026.34406)

### Inferences
- **Pattern E — "Related sources / more like this" panel** on every source and chunk view. NO-LLM: a kNN over stored document-level vectors (mean-pooled chunk vectors, or a dedicated summary vector). Can be blended with knowledge-graph overlap (shared entities, Jaccard) for explainability ("related because both mention Acme and Project X"). Grey needs: a document-level vector (or aggregation of chunk vectors), a `/similar?source_id=` API, and a UI panel.
- **Pattern F — next-best content** (what to read after this). NO-LLM if driven by co-view or co-retrieval logs (which sources are retrieved together in chats), like Algolia's collaborative-filtering approach. Grey has retrieval logs from Playground, widget, API and MCP, so a co-retrieval graph is a free by-product.
- **Pattern G — duplicate and near-duplicate report** at ingestion. NO-LLM: content hash for exact copies, MinHash on chunk text for near-copies, and a cosine threshold on document vectors for semantic duplicates. Shown as "This upload is 93% similar to X — skip, replace or keep both?". Useful for re-uploaded files and multiple transcripts of the same meeting. It also improves retrieval quality, because duplicates crowd out top-k.
- **Pattern H — contradiction detection** between near-duplicate chunks. **LLM** (or a small NLI model) is needed to decide whether two similar passages disagree. Intercom's ~15 contradictions per week shows it is valued. Run it only on candidate pairs found by the NO-LLM similarity step, to keep BYO costs small.
- Plagiarism as a product is a different market (education). For Grey, "overlap check of a draft against the project knowledge base" (paste text, see which sources it overlaps) is a cheap EMB feature that also suits Geiger Notes users.

### Gaps
- No vendor data was found on click-through or engagement lift from "related documents" panels in knowledge bases (as opposed to e-commerce).
- Official Copyleaks and Turnitin API pricing was not found.

## 3. Clustering and topic discovery: auto-generated topics or themes, taxonomy induction, trend detection over time

### Takeaway
The most commercially proven home for clustering is **customer-feedback analytics**. Unwrap, Enterpret, Thematic, Dovetail and Productboard Spark sell themes plus trends plus spike alerts, often for $20K+/yr. **Kraftful was acquired by Amplitude (July 2025).** The generic embedding-map topic product has not held up commercially: **Nomic has pivoted to an AEC (architecture, engineering, construction) agent platform**, Lilac was acquired by Databricks, and Apple open-sourced Embedding Atlas. The technique is commoditised (embeddings, then UMAP/HDBSCAN, then c-TF-IDF or LLM labels, as in BERTopic). Clustering and trend counting are LLM-free; only the human-readable labels and summaries need an LLM.

### Cited Findings
**Technique**
- BERTopic supports dynamic topic modelling via `.topics_over_time(docs, timestamps)` — [PyPI BERTopic 0.15.0](https://pypi.org/project/bertopic/0.15.0) — and LLM-generated topic labels through a representation model, stored separately via `.set_topic_labels` — [BERTopic docs](https://bertopic.readthedocs.io/); [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/bertopic)
- A 2024 study of Lithuanian election narratives found BERTopic kept topics more consistent across time steps than ANTM — [VU e-publications](https://epublications.vu.lt/object/elaba:238879873). DTECT (2025) adds LLM labelling to dynamic topic models from temporal keyword trajectories and works with LangChain-compatible models — [arXiv 2507.07910](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.07910v2)

**Feedback and theme analytics vendors**
- **Unwrap:** clusters and labels themes from unstructured text — [FitGap](https://us.fitgap.com/products/058941/unwrap-ai). Every theme links back to the original feedback — [The CX Lead review](https://thecxlead.com/da/tools/unwrap-anmeldelse/). Pricing is volume-based, starting at about **$24,000/yr** (listing data) with no free trial — [GetApp](https://www.getapp.com.au/software/2082335/unwrap); [FitGap](https://us.fitgap.com/products/058941/unwrap-ai). Reviewers note "limited value for low feedback volume teams" — [The CX Lead](https://thecxlead.com/da/tools/unwrap-anmeldelse/)
- **Unwrap spike rule (largely statistical):** a group is flagged at a 14-day high at least 25% above its prior peak that also beats its 2-, 7-, 14- and 30-day trailing averages, *and* clears one of: an all-time high, a day-over-day doubling, or several standard deviations above the mean. LLM "quality gates" (precision, coherence, urgency, duplicates) and deterministic spam filters run afterwards. Sensitivity is adjustable. Volume varies from several alerts a day to none in a week — [Unwrap docs, Realtime Alerts](https://docs.unwrap.ai/articles/3984222902-realtime-alerts). Average alert time is under 24 hours, delivered to Slack and email (vendor claim) — [Unwrap real-time alerts](https://www.unwrap.ai/real-time-alerts)
- **Enterpret:** an "adaptive taxonomy" builds categories from the feedback itself plus product docs and business context, sorts tickets, calls, surveys and reviews into a multi-level hierarchy, and updates as new feedback arrives (guide dated 23 Sep 2026) — [Enterpret](https://www.enterpret.com/guides/what-is-an-adaptive-taxonomy-in-customer-feedback). On 13 Oct 2026 it launched taxonomy management inside Enterpret Agent: the user asks how categories should change, sees everything affected, and nothing applies until approved — [Enterpret blog](https://www.enterpret.com/blog/enterpret-taxonomy-management)
- **Thematic:** Capterra lists "starting price $2000" with unclear period — [Capterra](https://capterra.com/p/159338/Thematic/). CleverX puts it at a custom $20K–$150K/yr — [CleverX](https://cleverx.com/blog/best-ai-tools-for-thematic-analysis-in-research-in-2026/)
- **Dovetail:** "AI clustering" ("cluster themes in data instantly and spot patterns without manual sorting") and AI dashboards on its pricing tiers — [Dovetail pricing](https://www.dovetail.com/pricing). Prices conflict: $99/mo+ — [CleverX](https://cleverx.com/blog/dovetail-review-2026/) — vs from $20/user/mo — [Techjockey](https://www.techjockey.com/us/detail/dovetail)
- **Productboard Spark:** July 2026 release notes describe key customer quotes surfaced in each feedback item's AI Insights, linkable to work items, plus natural-language scoping by company, theme or segment — [Productboard release notes](https://support.productboard.com/hc/en-us/articles/360060759874-Productboard-Release-Notes). Productboard claims "statistically accurate analysis… citations included", trend monitoring and VoC reports — [Productboard Spark](https://www.productboard.com/spark/)
- **Kraftful [ACQUIRED]:** acquired by Amplitude, announced 10 July 2025, and folded in as "AI Feedback" — [MarTech](https://martech.org/amplitude-acquires-kraftul-to-improve-customer-feedback-analysis/); [Business Wire](https://secure.businesswire.com/news/home/20250710876791/en/Amplitude-Acquires-Kraftful-to-Accelerate-AI-Strategy). A competitor blog says the standalone interface is offline — [BuildBetter](https://blog.buildbetter.ai/kraftful-acquired-by-amplitude-top-alternatives-for-product-teams/) (vendor source)
- **Kapa "Top Questions" / "Common Questions":** a search snippet of Kapa's docs says the Common Questions tab "automatically clusters by similarity all questions answered" — [Kapa docs](https://docs.kapa.ai/analytics/common-questions). Fetching the page returned Coverage Gaps content instead, so the exact behaviour is unconfirmed

**Embedding-map and topic-map products**
- **Nomic [PIVOTED]:** Nomic's docs now open with "a domain-specific AEC AI workspace and project delivery agent". Atlas appears only as a footer link ("Atlas Docs"), with no mention of data maps or topic modelling — [Nomic docs](https://docs.nomic.ai/). The pricing page lists Free / Individual $20/mo / Business $40/user/mo (25-seat minimum) / Enterprise and focuses on drawing review and document search for AE firms — [Nomic pricing](https://www.nomic.ai/pricing). It went live on the Autodesk Design & Make Marketplace on 7 Jul 2026 — [Nomic news](https://nomic.ai/news/nomic-autodesk-aeco-partner-design-and-make-marketplace) — and took strategic investment from Arcadis and Aurecon in Sept 2026 — [Arcadis](https://www.arcadis.com/en/news/global/2026/arcadis-accelerates-ai-enabled-delivery-through-investment-in-nomic). Atlas had tiered pricing (Free/Plus/Business/Enterprise) from Oct 2024 — [UsagePricing](https://www.usagepricing.com/blueprint/activity/nomic-2026-08-11-individual-tier-launch)
- **Lilac [ACQUIRED]:** Databricks acquired Lilac (search, cluster and analyse text datasets) in March 2024 to fold it into Mosaic AI — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/databricks-acquires-lilac-to-supercharge-data-quality-efforts-for-gen-ai-apps); [InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/3714681/databricks-acquires-lilac-ai-to-boost-data-quality-for-llm-training.html)
- **Apple Embedding Atlas (open source, May 2025):** in-browser visualisation, cross-filtering and search of embeddings and metadata, with automatic clustering and labelling, density contours, real-time nearest-neighbour search, and WebGPU (WebGL 2 fallback) for up to a few million points — [arXiv 2505.06386](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.06386); [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2025/11/embedding-atlas); [Landscape listing](https://landscape.jimmysong.io/projects/embedding-atlas/). About 3.4K GitHub stars when captured — [ecosyste.ms](https://science.ecosyste.ms/projects/4046)

### Inferences
- **Pattern I — auto-topics for a project** ("your knowledge base covers these 14 themes"). Clustering is NO-LLM (HDBSCAN or k-means on stored chunk or document vectors). Labels can be NO-LLM (c-TF-IDF keywords, or the most central entities from the knowledge graph in each cluster) or optionally LLM for nicer names. Grey needs: a periodic clustering job per project, a cluster table (cluster_id, centroid, members, label), and a topics browse page that lists sources per topic.
- **Pattern J — taxonomy induction** (hierarchical topics, editable). Enterpret's 13 Oct 2026 launch shows the current UX: AI proposes, human approves, with an impact preview. Hierarchy is NO-LLM (agglomerative clustering of centroids). Approvals and renames are human. Merge/split suggestions are optional LLM.
- **Pattern K — trend detection over time.** NO-LLM: per-topic and per-entity counts by ingestion or document date, with spike rules like Unwrap's (trailing averages plus statistical thresholds). Unwrap uses LLMs only as a post-filter, which shows the detection core needs none. Grey sources carry timestamps, so "topics rising this month" and "entity mentions over time" are cheap.
- **Pattern L — question clustering** (what users ask, not what docs contain). NO-LLM clustering over chat and query logs, plus optional LLM summaries, as with Kapa Top Questions and Coverage Gaps. Grey's chat endpoints already produce these logs.
- Commercial lesson: willingness to pay sits with **vertical use cases** (VoC and product feedback at $20K+/yr), not with generic "visualise your embeddings" tools (Nomic pivoted, Lilac absorbed). For Grey, topics should appear as a built-in tab of a project, not a standalone analytics product. Specific packaging could follow later, such as a "feedback analysis" template when a project's sources are support tickets or interviews.

### Gaps
- **Viable (askviable.com):** status could not be checked (search budget exhausted). No evidence of shutdown or acquisition was found in one search.
- Amplitude AI Feedback's current scope and pricing were not verified (the Business Wire page returned 403).
- No independent accuracy benchmarks for vendor theme detection were found. Most claims are vendor marketing.
- Whether Nomic Atlas still accepts new data-map customers is unclear. The product name survives only as a docs footer link.

## 4. Embedding-map visualisations and knowledge-graph exploration UIs: what do people actually use graph views for?

### Takeaway
Graph exploration sells in **investigation-heavy, high-price niches**: fraud/AML, law enforcement and intelligence, and corporate investigations (Linkurious/Nuix, Neo4j Bloom, Kineviz GraphXR). In personal knowledge management, the global graph view is widely seen as decorative. Real value comes from the **local (one-hop) graph**, filters and spotting orphans and gaps. GraphRAG-style community summaries are the main LLM-powered "explore the whole corpus" idea, but **Microsoft GraphRAG is in maintenance mode**. Graph views, graph analytics (centrality, communities, gaps) and embedding maps all run on stored graph or vector data, with **no LLM** needed.

### Cited Findings
**What people use graph views for**
- Obsidian forum users say the full graph "has become completely useless as the number of notes has grown", node positions shift on every load, and the only use one found was identifying unlinked (orphan) notes — [Obsidian forum: "What's the point of the graph view?"](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/whats-the-point-of-the-graph-view-how-are-you-using-it/71316); [Obsidian forum: "How do you use the graph view?"](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/how-do-you-use-the-graph-view/2785)
- Defenders use it to check vault health: stray unlinked notes at the edges signal that indexes need updating, and floating clusters suggest a folder was never integrated. Several prefer the **local graph** ("a fancy backlink view"). ### Inferences
- **Pattern M — entity-neighbourhood (local) graph view**: click an entity and see its one- or two-hop relations with source citations on each edge. NO-LLM (reads Grey's existing entity/relation tables). Evidence from Obsidian and Cognee favours *local, query-scoped* graphs over a global hairball. Grey's Playground "context view" could add "show retrieved subgraph" next to retrieved chunks, as Cognee's Graph Explorer does.
- **Pattern N — graph analytics panel**: most central entities (PageRank or degree), communities (Louvain/Leiden), bridging entities (betweenness), orphan entities, and **structural gaps** between communities (InfraNodus-style). All NO-LLM, computable in-database or with a JS/Python graph library. Optional LLM community summaries (GraphRAG-style) are cost-heavy, and the reference implementation is now in maintenance mode, so make them on-demand per community rather than a full precompute.
- **Pattern O — embedding map** (2D UMAP of chunks or sources, coloured by topic, source type or date, with lasso selection and search). NO-LLM. Grey could embed Apple's open-source Embedding Atlas component rather than build its own. Commercial evidence says this works as a feature, not a product.
- **Pattern P — investigation UX** (paths between two entities, timeline filter, saved views). NO-LLM path queries. This is relevant if Grey targets research, legal or analyst users. Linkurious and GraphXR show that buyers pay thousands per month, but they need data-governance features too.
- Expect power users, not the majority, to use graph views. Position them as a "debug and explore your knowledge" tool and an ingestion-quality check (bad or duplicate entity extractions show up visually), which also helps Grey users trust knowledge-graph extraction.

## 5. Knowledge health and gap analytics: unanswered questions, content gaps, stale or conflicting content, coverage reports

### Takeaway
"Questions your docs can't answer" is now a standard paid analytics tier for AI-support and docs-AI vendors: Kapa Coverage Gaps, Intercom Fin Recommendations (Pro add-on), Inkeep AI Reports (Enterprise), Guru verification, and Document360 zero-result searches. The pipeline is: flag uncertain or failed answers, cluster them (NO-LLM), then LLM-write a finding and recommendation. A cautionary data point is that **Zendesk retired Content Cues (May 2025) for "very low utilization"**, so gap insights need a workflow (assign, resolve, ticket export), not just a report.

### Cited Findings
- **Kapa Coverage Gaps:** for a calendar week, month or quarter, Kapa analyses all "Uncertain" answers (the label it applies when sources don't cover the topic), clusters recurring topics, and writes a **Finding** and a **Recommendation**. It needs a minimum volume of uncertain conversations per period before clusters appear. It differs from Top Questions, which analyses all questions — [Kapa docs, Coverage Gaps](https://docs.kapa.ai/analytics/coverage-gaps). The UI supports a CSV export of clusters with their conversations and stepping back and forward through periods. It counts the first question per uncertain thread, and recommendations need human review — [Kapa docs](https://docs.kapa.ai/analytics/common-questions) (fetched page content). Language filtering on analytics is not supported — [Kapa FAQ](https://docs.kapa.ai/analytics/faq)
- Kapa blog (byline 10 Feb 2026): the "I don't know" guardrail flags gaps. The dashboard shows conversations processed and gaps identified, with a status tag per gap and drill-down to the transcript and sources used. Kapa's own top gap (users asking about Google Drive) led it to build that integration — [Kapa blog](https://www.kapa.ai/blog/from-“i-don’t-know”-to-insight-using-ai-to-find-documentation-gaps). Marketing pages mention scheduled reports and pushing gaps to Jira, Linear and GitHub — [Kapa Analyze](https://www.kapa.ai/product/analyze)
- **Kapa pricing:** "tailored", platform fee plus answers per month — [Kapa pricing](https://kapa.ai/pricing). Vendr median $20,700/yr (11 purchases) — [Vendr](https://www.vendr.com/marketplace/kapa-ai). Another review cites a median of $25,200/yr, with a range of about $12K–$83K — [Fern](https://buildwithfern.com/post/kapa-reviews-pricing-alternatives)
- **Intercom Fin Recommendations:** need the **Pro add-on**, found under Fin AI Agent > Analyze > Recommendations, filtered by "Reason is Content gaps". Sources: failed Fin replies compared with successful human replies, teammate-handled conversations, duplicates, and contradictions across sources. Weekly batch capped at 20 (about 15 contradictions, about 5 duplicates). Excludes abandoned chats, conversations without teammate replies, and feature requests or bugs. The UI shows AI-drafted create/edit proposals, the triggering conversations, and direct article or snippet updates. Pending items expire after 4 weeks — [Intercom help](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/11394959-use-ai-powered-content-suggestions-to-improve-fin)
- **Inkeep:** "AI Reports on Knowledge Gaps" and "AI Reports on Product Feature Gaps" are **Enterprise-only** (preview deployment of the pricing page) — [Inkeep pricing (preview)](https://agents-docs-7mhm5hg0a.preview.inkeep.com/pricing). Insights export conversation-level insights, feedback and identified gaps — [Inkeep for product teams](https://inkeep.com/use-cases/product-teams). Cloud pricing is quote-based — [Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/inkeep-ai/pricing)
- **Zendesk Content Cues [RETIRED]:** launched for Guide Enterprise in 2021 using Answer Bot ML to suggest articles to create or update from tickets — [Zendesk blog](https://www.zendesk.co.uk/blog/introducing-content-cues-for-zendesk-guide-enterprise/). Removal began 1 May 2025 due to "very low utilization… minimal perceived value" — [Zendesk announcement](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/8558652714778). A third party says the successor is a "Knowledge Base Gaps Discovery" add-on that reads bot conversations, plus Explore reports on zero-result searches — [eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/zendesk-knowledge-gap-identify-missing-articles) (not confirmed officially)
- **Guru:** verification is the core mechanism. Stale cards go to an expert for re-approval, and duplicates, conflicts and gaps are flagged as content is used — [Guru help, verification](https://help.getguru.com/docs/verifying-and-unverifying-cards); [KMWorld](https://www.kmworld.com/Articles/Editorial/Features/Guru-the-AI-source-of-truth-for-enterprise-knowledge-176368.aspx). Knowledge Agents apply behavioural rules (usage) and content rules (dates, evergreen-ness). Unverify rules run before verify rules, and auto-verify is on by default for agents created after Jan 2026 — reported in search summaries of Guru material ([Guru](https://www.getguru.com/)); exact page not confirmed. Vendor case study: Cartwheel Care went from 60% to 100% verified — [Guru](https://www.getguru.com/)
- **Document360:** no-result searches, search success rate, bounced searches, per-article views, reads, reading time and ratings; Link Status and 404 analytics on Business and Enterprise plans — [Document360 search analytics](https://docs.document360.com/docs/analytics-search.md); [Document360 analytics](https://docs.document360.com/docs/analytics.md). Critique: strong at showing underperforming articles, weaker at showing an article is *wrong* — [Enterpret guide, Aug 2026](https://www.enterpret.com/guides/the-6-best-tools-to-find-help-center-articles-that-are-out-of-date-or-wrong-in-2026) (competitor source)
- **Prevalence claims (unreliable):** a vendor blog cites Gartner as saying about 70% of enterprise KBs contain at least one directly contradictory pair — [Fini](https://www.usefini.com/guides/ai-knowledge-base-conflicting-answers) — and another Fini page cites 47% of articles as outdated, duplicated or contradicted — [Fini](https://www.usefini.com/guides/top-ai-tools-knowledge-base-management). The two conflict and neither is confirmed against Gartner.
- **InfraNodus** frames "content gaps" as structural holes between topic clusters, a graph-only technique — [InfraNodus](https://infranodus.com/docs/content-gap-analysis)

### Inferences
- **Pattern Q — unanswered-question report.** Detection can be NO-LLM: top-k retrieval similarity below a threshold, or no knowledge-graph entity match, on every Playground, widget, chat-completions, `/retrieve` or MCP call. An LLM-judged "uncertain" flag is optional. Clustering of those queries is NO-LLM. Finding and recommendation text is LLM, so make it on-demand. Grey needs: a query and answer log with retrieval scores (it likely has part of this from the Playground context view), a periodic clustering job, and a "Gaps" tab with status and owner, CSV export, and an "Add source to fill this gap" action. Zendesk's retirement suggests closing the loop (upload or link a source, re-test the question) matters more than the report itself.
- **Pattern R — coverage report.** NO-LLM: map question clusters onto source-topic clusters (Section 3) to show topics asked about but thinly covered, and topics covered but never asked about (dead content).
- **Pattern S — staleness.** NO-LLM: source age, last re-sync time for web sources, and sources never retrieved in N days ("dead weight"). Optional LLM checks for date-bound statements ("as of 2024…"), as in Guru's content rules.
- **Pattern T — conflict detection.** LLM or NLI on candidate pairs only. Candidate pairs come from NO-LLM similarity (Section 2) or from knowledge-graph edges where the same subject and relation has different objects across sources (e.g. Acme–CEO–Alice vs Acme–CEO–Bob). The knowledge-graph route is a cheap, structured conflict detector most chat-only vendors lack. Graphiti-style `invalid_at` (Section 6) is the natural way to record which fact superseded which.
- **Pattern U — retrieval-health metrics** per pipeline: share of queries with weak matches, sources never cited, most-cited sources, and citation concentration. NO-LLM. Useful for Grey's per-pipeline OpenAI-compatible endpoints, because operators can see what their API consumers ask.
- Commercial signal: gap analytics are gated to higher tiers (Intercom Pro add-on, Inkeep Enterprise, Kapa paid). This suggests it is a pricing lever. Under BYO, Grey can offer the NO-LLM version broadly and the LLM-written recommendations on the user's own key.

### Gaps
- No published data on how often customers act on gap recommendations (e.g. share of Intercom suggestions accepted).
- Zendesk's current official gap tooling was not confirmed. All details come from third parties.
- Exact Guru documentation for the Jan-2026 auto-verify default was not located.

## 6. Entity-centric views: auto-generated entity pages or profiles aggregating every mention across sources, with per-entity timelines

### Takeaway
Auto-generated entity and topic pages are technically easy on a knowledge-graph stack, but there is a **notable commercial failure**: **Microsoft Viva Topics (AI-generated topic pages over SharePoint) was retired on 22 Feb 2025**, and its AI-generated pages were removed. Surviving forms are narrower: Glean's people-centric graph, code-repo wikis (Cognition DeepWiki), investigation timelines (Linkurious), and temporal fact graphs for agent memory (Zep Graphiti). A "mentions list plus timeline plus relations" entity page is NO-LLM. Only the summary paragraph needs an LLM.

### Cited Findings
- **Microsoft Viva Topics [RETIRED]:** retired 22 Feb 2025. AI-generated topic pages are gone; only user-published topic pages survive, as ordinary SharePoint pages without AI enhancements. The Topic Center becomes a normal SharePoint site. Microsoft now recommends publishing knowledge in SharePoint for discovery via Microsoft Search and Copilot — [Microsoft Learn, Viva Topics](https://learn.microsoft.com/viva/topics); [Topics changes FAQ](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/topics/topics-changes-faq?view=o365-worldwide). New feature development stopped 22 Feb 2024 — [ClearPeople](https://www.clearpeople.com/blog/viva-topics-retired-faqs)
- **Glean:** the "people" pillar builds "a consolidated view of each person across all connected applications", with roles, teams and relationships. The `from` filter finds people associated with a document — [Glean knowledge graph](https://docs.glean.com/security/knowledge-graph); [Glean search guide](https://docs.glean.com/user-guide/search/how-to-search-in-glean)
- **Cognition DeepWiki:** launched April 2025. It auto-generates wiki pages for GitHub repos (swap `github.com` for `deepwiki.com`) and identifies concepts using symbol and call graphs. Pages are generated independently, so duplicate content can appear across pages — [x-cmd blog](https://www.x-cmd.com/blog/250502); [Codersera guide](https://codersera.com/blog/deepwiki-complete-guide-2026/amp/) (third-party). An MCP bundle exposes the hierarchical table of contents, topic pages and Q&A — [MCPBundles](https://www.mcpbundles.com/skills/deepwiki-mcp)
- **"LLM wiki" pattern:** a Claude Code plugin implements Karpathy's idea of an LLM-maintained knowledge base, in which ingesting a source integrates its information into persistent markdown pages — [deep-wiki plugin](https://www.claudepluginhub.com/plugins/sungmin-cho-deep-wiki)
- **Zep Graphiti (temporal knowledge graph):** bi-temporal, tracking event time and ingestion time. Edges carry `created_at`, `expired_at`, `valid_at` and `invalid_at`. A contradicted fact is invalidated, not deleted, which preserves history. Temporal filtering in search was "planned" in an older post — [Zep blog](https://blog.getzep.com/beyond-static-knowledge-graphs/); [graphiti-core PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/graphiti-core/0.7.7/); [Zep paper summary, Jul 2026](https://signals.aktagon.com/articles/2026/07/zep-a-temporal-knowledge-graph-architecture-for-agent-memory/)
- **Linkurious:** a timeline over entities and events is a core investigator feature — [Linkurious](https://linkurious.com/law-enforcement-and-intelligence-solution/)
- **Obsidian:** users treat the local graph as "a fancy backlink view" (an entity page built from links) — [Eleanor Konik](https://www.eleanorkonik.com/its-not-just-a-pretty-gimmick-in-defense-of-obsidians-graph-view)

### Inferences
- **Pattern V — entity page:** canonical name and aliases; type; every mention with a snippet and source link (sortable by date and source type); related entities ranked by co-occurrence or edge weight; a mention-count timeline; and an optional LLM summary. Everything except the summary is NO-LLM over Grey's existing knowledge-graph tables. Grey needs: entity resolution and merge tools (alias management; human merge/split, as Guru keeps merges under edit permissions), an entity index page with search and type filters, and per-entity "subscribe" (Section 7).
- **Pattern W — per-entity timeline of facts:** requires time on edges. Grey could adopt Graphiti-style `valid_at`/`invalid_at` on relations (document date as event time, ingestion time as transaction time). This also powers conflict and supersession display in Section 5.
- Why Viva Topics failed (inference, not documented by Microsoft): enterprise topic mining across a whole tenant produced many low-quality pages that needed curation, and the value moved into Copilot chat. Grey's risk is lower because projects are small and user-curated, but the lesson holds. Entity pages should be **generated on demand from data, not as a precomputed wiki needing curation**, and LLM summaries should be cached and clearly marked as generated.
- Demand signal is mixed: Glean (people) and DeepWiki (code) show entity and wiki views are valued in specific domains, and Viva Topics shows generic auto-topic pages struggle.

### Gaps
- No current (2026) commercial product was found that ships generic auto-generated entity profile pages over arbitrary uploaded documents with public pricing. Searching for this mostly returned SEO "entity optimisation" content.
- Microsoft's official reasons for retiring Viva Topics were not stated in the retrieved pages beyond the move to SharePoint and Copilot.

## 7. Alerts and monitoring: notify when new content matches a saved query, entity or topic; competitive or regulatory monitoring over ingested feeds

### Takeaway
Saved-search alerts are a long-proven, mostly **LLM-free** pattern: the Elasticsearch percolator inverts search to match new documents against millions of stored queries. Commercial monitoring products charge heavily for it on curated feeds: Feedly Market and Threat Intelligence about $14–19K/yr, AlphaSense often $17K–$125K/yr, Klue and Crayon about $20–40K/yr. They add spike detection (Unwrap) and AI relevance scoring (Crayon). Grey already ingests web pages and other sources, so "notify me when a new source matches X / mentions entity Y / joins topic Z" is a natural, cheap extension.

### Cited Findings
- **Elasticsearch percolator:** stores queries in a `percolator` field and percolates each new document to find matching saved searches. REA Group (realestate.com.au) matches thousands of new listings a day against millions of saved searches for daily notifications — [Elastic community event](https://community.elastic.co/events/details/elastic-mumbai-presents-elastic-percolation-to-match-new-real-estate-listings-against-saved-searches/); [OneUptime percolate guide (Jan 2026)](https://oneuptime.com/blog/post/2026-01-27-elasticsearch-percolate-queries/view)
- **Graphlit [WINDING DOWN]:** "alerts" were a scheduled form of publishing that ran queries and processing on a recurring schedule, posting to Slack or a webhook. Feeds synced from 30+ sources (Slack, Gmail, GitHub, S3, RSS) — [Graphlit key concepts](https://docs.graphlit.dev/graphlit-platform/key-concepts); [Graphlit overview](https://docs.graphlit.dev/getting-started/overview). LLM entity extraction was billed by tokens — [Graphlit extraction docs](https://docs.graphlit.dev/graphlit-data-api/api-reference/workflows/extraction). Earlier third-party pricing tiers: Hobby $49 / Starter $199 / Growth $999 per month — [EveryDev](https://www.everydev.ai/tools/graphlit)
- **Feedly:** Feedly for Market Intelligence $14,400/yr and Feedly for Threat Intelligence $19,200/yr (flat-rate) — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/202497/Feedly/pricing/). Consumer Pro+ with the Leo AI assistant is about $99/yr — [Readless](https://www.readless.app/blog/feedly-pro-pricing-vs-readless-2026); other reviews give different figures. Leo only works within sources the user has added, and advanced AI topic models are reserved for Enterprise — [Readless](https://www.readless.app/blog/feedly-pro-pricing-vs-readless-2026)
- **AlphaSense:** saved searches feed automated email alerts containing a snippet of each result. Smart Synonyms expand queries — [AlphaSense blog](https://www.alpha-sense.com/blog/product/time-saving-tips/). No public pricing. Third-party estimates: $10K–$100K per user per year — [CostBench](https://costbench.com/software/financial-data-terminals/alphasense/free-plan/); averages of $44,754/yr (SMB) and $125,124/yr (enterprise) — [SpendHound](https://www.spendhound.com/marketplace/alphasense-pricing). Annual only, no free plan — [PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/alphasense)
- **Klue / Crayon (competitive intelligence):** both quote-only. Vendr benchmarks cited in roundups put Crayon at $25K–$40K/yr and Klue at $20K–$40K/yr mid-market. Crayon uses AI to score signals by strategic relevance across 100+ data types and delivers alerts, battlecards and dashboards. Klue reportedly acquired Ignition in Sept 2025 — [Fast.io roundup](https://www.fast.io/resources/best-ai-competitive-intelligence-tools-2026.md); [Autobound roundup](https://www.autobound.ai/blog/top-15-competitive-intelligence-tools-2026) (secondary sources)
- **Unwrap** spike alerts: statistical detection, then LLM gates (see Section 3) — [Unwrap docs](https://docs.unwrap.ai/articles/3984222902-realtime-alerts)
- **Kapa:** scheduled analytics reports — [Kapa Analyze](https://www.kapa.ai/product/analyze) (marketing page, via search snippet)

### Inferences
- **Pattern X — saved query alerts:** the user saves a query, filters or a semantic description. On each ingestion, Grey compares new chunk embeddings (already computed during ingestion) with saved-query embeddings (embedded once) and keyword-matches with a percolator-style reverse index. NO-LLM per new document. Grey needs: a `saved_searches` table, a post-ingest hook, a threshold per alert, and delivery by email, webhook or Slack (Geiger Notify, the `@geiger/notify` package in Grey's dependencies, is a natural fit), plus a digest mode (daily or weekly).
- **Pattern Y — entity watch:** "tell me when a new source mentions Acme or a new relation is added to Acme". NO-LLM, triggered by knowledge-graph upserts that ingestion already produces.
- **Pattern Z — topic and trend alerts:** a new cluster emerges, or a topic or entity spikes compared with trailing averages (Unwrap's rule). NO-LLM detection, with an optional LLM digest summary on the user's key.
- **Pattern AA — monitored feeds:** scheduled re-crawl of URLs, RSS or sitemaps into a project, plus alerts. This is the Feedly, AlphaSense and Klue shape. The price points ($14K–$40K/yr) show high willingness to pay for competitive and regulatory monitoring, though buyers pay for the curated content sources as much as the alerting. For Grey this needs scheduled connectors and change detection (diff of re-crawled pages, which is NO-LLM). A "what changed" summary would be LLM.
- Under BYO, the expensive part of commercial monitoring (LLM relevance scoring of every item) can be replaced by embedding similarity thresholds, keeping per-item cost near zero.

### Gaps
- No official prices for Klue, Crayon or AlphaSense. All figures are third-party estimates.
- No quantitative data on alert fatigue or engagement (open rates of saved-search digests) in knowledge tools.
- Regulatory-monitoring vendors (e.g. Regology) were not researched (search budget exhausted).

## 8. Embeddings and retrieval as a raw data service: exporting vectors, similarity APIs, using a KB as features for downstream ML

### Takeaway
Few managed RAG vendors expose raw vectors. **Vectara explicitly does not allow exporting raw embeddings**, and supports only document and chunk text plus metadata, with no bulk export. A self-hosted or BYO-embedding stack can expose vectors, which makes "your knowledge base as a data asset" (vector export, similarity and cluster APIs, embeddings as ML features) a differentiator for Grey. It is LLM-free, and because users chose their own embedding model, exported vectors stay usable in their own downstream tools (Apple Embedding Atlas, BERTopic, classifiers).

### Cited Findings
- **Vectara data egress:** "raw embeddings/vectors" and original uploaded files cannot be exported. Export means iterating corpora, then documents, then document parts (chunks with positions and metadata), because "bulk export operations are not currently available" — [Vectara docs](https://docs.vectara.com/docs/data-management/data-egress)
- Self-hosted pgvector setups can export embeddings directly. One community package offers CSV export — [@sf-ai/rag-functions](https://www.jsdelivr.com/package/npm/@sf-ai/rag-functions)
- **Nomic Embed:** open weights, free to run locally; API/Enterprise pricing on request (third-party) — [CostBench](https://costbench.com/software/embedding-apis/nomic-embed/). Grey already has a native Nomic embedding provider (repo commit a8bb620).
- Downstream consumers of exported vectors exist: Apple Embedding Atlas visualises embeddings in-browser — [arXiv 2505.06386](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.06386). BERTopic accepts precomputed embeddings for topic modelling — [BERTopic docs](https://bertopic.readthedocs.io/). Lilac (now Databricks) clustered text datasets for RAG and fine-tuning data prep — [InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/3714681/databricks-acquires-lilac-ai-to-boost-data-quality-for-llm-training.html)
- **Retrieval as an agent tool:** InfraNodus exposes its graph and gap analysis over MCP and API for agents — [InfraNodus MCP](https://infranodus.com/use-case/mcp-server). Cognition's DeepWiki exposes its generated wiki over MCP — [MCPBundles](https://www.mcpbundles.com/skills/deepwiki-mcp). Kapa exports gap clusters as CSV — [Kapa docs](https://docs.kapa.ai/analytics/coverage-gaps)
- **Data portability as a selling point:** InfraNodus markets data as "private, exportable" — [InfraNodus](https://infranodus.com/)

### Inferences
- **Pattern AB — vector and chunk export** (Parquet or JSONL: chunk_id, source_id, text, metadata, entity ids, vector, model name and dimension). NO-LLM. Grey needs: an export job, plus model provenance on every vector, so consumers know which BYO model produced it.
- **Pattern AC — similarity and cluster API:** `/similar` (by text, chunk, source or entity), `/clusters`, `/topics-over-time`, `/entities/{id}/mentions`, `/graph/neighbors`. NO-LLM except embedding a free-text input. Exposing these as **MCP tools** next to Grey's existing retrieval MCP lets external agents do discovery and analytics without Grey paying for any generation.
- **Pattern AD — knowledge base as ML features:** per-source vectors and cluster or entity memberships as features for classifiers (e.g. routing tickets to topics). NO-LLM. Classification can use nearest-centroid on Grey's own clusters, giving "auto-tag new uploads into existing topics" with no LLM.
- **Pattern AE — graph export** (entities and relations as CSV, GraphML or Cypher) so users can open the graph in Neo4j Bloom, GraphXR or Gephi. NO-LLM. This supports Section 4 without Grey building every visualisation.
- Demand for raw vector export is inferred, not measured. The evidence is indirect (vendors that lock vectors in; open tools that consume them).

#### Cross-pattern synthesis (applies to all eight questions)
**LLM-free patterns (embeddings, graph, metadata or counts only; cheap under BYO):**
- A hybrid search results page (EMB per query, or pure BM25)
- Facets by source, type, date and entity
- Search analytics and zero-result or weak-match queries
- Autocomplete from query logs and entity names
- Related sources / more-like-this
- Co-retrieval "next-best content"
- Exact, near and semantic duplicate detection
- Auto-topic clustering with c-TF-IDF or entity labels
- Hierarchical taxonomy proposals
- Topic and entity trend counts and spike detection
- Question clustering from chat logs
- Coverage maps (questions vs content)
- Staleness and dead-content reports
- Knowledge-graph-structured conflict candidates (same subject and relation, different object)
- Retrieval-health metrics
- Entity pages (mentions, relations, timeline)
- Local graph views, graph analytics (centrality, communities, structural gaps), path finding
- Embedding maps (UMAP)
- Saved-query, entity and topic alerts, plus page-change detection
- Vector, chunk and graph export; similarity, cluster and graph APIs and MCP tools
- Nearest-centroid auto-tagging

**Patterns that need LLM calls (on the user's key, ideally on demand):** gap Finding/Recommendation write-ups (Kapa- and Intercom-style), contradiction verdicts on candidate pairs, nicer cluster and taxonomy names, entity and community summaries (GraphRAG-style), alert digests and "what changed" summaries, and LLM quality gates on alerts.

**Most commercially proven, ranked by evidence of paid demand:**
1. Search with facets plus Ask AI in one box (Algolia, Vertex AI Search at $1.50–$4 per 1K queries, Glean, Coveo, Meilisearch from $20/mo)
2. Knowledge-gap and content-health analytics (Kapa at a median of about $20–25K/yr, Intercom Pro add-on, Inkeep Enterprise, Guru, Document360). Zendesk Content Cues' retirement is a warning against report-only designs.
3. Theme clustering and trend detection over feedback (Unwrap at about $24K/yr, Thematic at $20–150K/yr, Enterpret, Dovetail, Productboard Spark; Kraftful acquired by Amplitude)
4. Monitoring and alerts over feeds (Feedly at $14–19K/yr, AlphaSense, Klue and Crayon at $20–40K/yr; the percolator pattern at REA scale)
5. Recommendations and related content (Algolia Recommend, Elastic kNN); proven in e-commerce, less measured in knowledge bases
6. Duplicate and contradiction hygiene (Guru Duplicate Detection, Intercom's ~20 suggestions a week)
7. Graph exploration for investigations (Linkurious/Nuix, Neo4j Bloom, GraphXR at about $5–15K/mo); a high-price niche, while consumer graph views are mostly decorative

**Weak or failed as standalone products:** generic embedding-map tools (Nomic pivoted to AEC, Lilac absorbed into Databricks, Apple open-sourced Embedding Atlas) and auto-generated topic/entity pages (Viva Topics retired). Ship these as features inside Grey, not as headline products.

### Gaps
- No vendor-neutral evidence on how many knowledge-base customers request raw vector export.
- Ragie, Pinecone Assistant and other managed RAG vendors' export policies were not checked (search budget exhausted). Only Vectara's policy is confirmed.
- No data on BYO embedding model drift: what happens to exports and clusters when a user switches embedding models. This is a design question Grey will need to answer (re-embed vs keep multiple vector versions).
