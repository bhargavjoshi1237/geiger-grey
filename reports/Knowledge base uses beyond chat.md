# Put Grey's knowledge to work beyond chat

Grey's most valuable step beyond chat is not another conversational surface. It is a typed **task**: a saved definition that pairs an output schema (a label set, a field list, a verdict or a document template) with a retrieval scope, the customer's own model, mandatory citations and a human review state, and that runs once, across a document × question grid, on every new source, or on a schedule. That one primitive covers the non-chat uses with the strongest commercial evidence: grids (Harvey at a reported ~$300M ARR, Legora past $100M ARR in about 18 months), schema extraction (eight-plus vendors and all three hyperscalers publish per-page prices), questionnaire answering (Loopio's median contract is about $23.7K a year) and classification for routing (Intercom, Zendesk). A second family needs no generative model at all: faceted search, related sources, duplicate checks, knowledge-gap analytics and alerts. Under bring-your-own (BYO), that family costs almost nothing per operation, so it should ship first. BYO helps Grey because Grey carries no token risk on recurring or bulk work. In exchange, cost control and reliability move into the product: pre-run estimates, hard caps, a model choice per task, and Grey-side validation of structured output, because providers enforce JSON schemas unevenly. The codebase already holds most of the parts (an LLM-free hybrid `searchChunks`, a structured-output extractor, an eval runner shaped like a grid, a lease-based worker queue, an MCP tool registry and zero-cost BYOK metering), and sibling apps already ship signed webhooks, a Flow-issue writer and a suite notification sender. What Grey lacks is four foundations: retrieval scoped below the knowledge base, a generic task and job model, tables for derived outputs with review state, and any outbound channel. The recommended order is scoped search, gap analytics and notifications first; then the task primitive with classify and extract; then the grid; then standing rules, webhooks, alerts and write-back into Flow and Forms; and finally generated artifacts and questionnaire answering. Standalone embedding maps, precomputed topic pages and head-on support agents have weak or negative track records and should stay off the roadmap.

## Grids, extraction and questionnaires hold the strongest commercial proof

Across roughly forty non-chat patterns researched, paid demand concentrates where a knowledge base produces **structured, reviewable output inside a workflow**. It is weakest where a product only offers a new way to look at the same content. The clearest case is the document × question grid. Documents (or companies) become rows, prompts become typed columns, and each cell holds a short answer, its reasoning, numbered citations and a verify-or-flag state. A human edit always overrides the AI value. Harvey's review tables work exactly this way, expose an asynchronous `add_row` API, and accept up to 10,000 files per table ([Harvey developer guide](https://developers.harvey.ai/guides/review_tables); [Harvey Vault](https://help.harvey.ai/articles/vault)). The companies built on this surface are among the fastest-growing in AI. Harvey reports a **$15.5B valuation and about $300M ARR by May 2026** ([Entrepreneurloop](https://entrepreneurloop.com/harvey-ai-valuation-15-5b/)). Legora passed **$100M ARR in about 18 months** ([TNW](https://thenextweb.com:443/news/legora-just-hit-100-million-in-revenue-it-took-18-months)). Rogo is valued at about **$2B and serves 250+ banks and investment firms** ([FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/04/29/rogo-raises-160m-series-d-to-scale-finance-ai-platform/)). Hebbia's Matrix sells for an estimated **$3,000–$10,000 per seat per year** ([Metronome](https://metronome.com/pricing-index/hebbia)). These figures come from secondary sources and conflict at the margins, and no vendor discloses what share of usage is the grid rather than chat. The same pattern has also gone horizontal at far lower prices, as Airtable field agents, Notion AI autofill, Excel's `=COPILOT()` and Clay's AI columns ([Airtable](https://support.airtable.com/docs/using-airtable-ai-in-fields); [Microsoft](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/excel/copilot-function)). So the shape is not confined to legal buyers paying $10K a seat.

Schema extraction is the most transparently priced pattern. List prices cluster at **$5–$50 per 1,000 pages with the model included**. Extend charges $7–$50 depending on tier ([Extend](https://www.extend.ai/pricing.md)), Reducto $20–$40 ([Reducto](https://reducto.ai/pricing)), Google's custom extractor $30 ([Google](https://cloud.google.com/document-ai)), and AWS Bedrock Data Automation $0.04 per page for custom output ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/bedrock/pricing/)). Azure Content Understanding is the outlier that matters for Grey. It charges **$1–$3 per 1,000 pages for grounding and confidence and bills every LLM token to the customer's own deployment** ([Azure](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/content-understanding/pricing-explainer)), which is the BYO model in all but name. Extraction also ships as a standing rule that fires on every new file. Box Extract went GA in January 2026 ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260115566865/en)). SharePoint autofill columns cost **$0.005 per page** ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/microsoft-365/syntex/autofill-overview)). Ragie runs natural-language "instructions" with a JSON schema on every created or updated document ([Ragie](https://docs.ragie.ai/docs/entity-extraction)).

Older "answer from my knowledge" businesses confirm the pattern. Security-questionnaire and RFP tools turn a question list into drafted, cited answers for human approval. Loopio's median contract is about **$23,700 a year** ([KI-Syndikat, citing Vendr](https://www.ki-syndikat.de/tools/loopio/)), and Drata paid about **$250M for SafeBase** ([SC World](https://www.scworld.com/brief/drata-acquires-safebase-for-250m)). Classification against a fixed label set is proven inside support suites. Intercom's Fin Attributes tag each conversation with admin-defined values that drive escalation rules, workflows and reporting ([Intercom](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/11680403-fin-attributes-beta)). Data platforms sell it as typed functions, such as Databricks `ai_classify`, which takes label descriptions and returns confidence and rationale ([Databricks](https://docs.databricks.com/api/ai-functions/v1/ai-classify)). The largest pool of money sits in support automation grounded in a knowledge base. Salesforce agreed to buy Fin (formerly Intercom) for about **$3.6B** ([MarTech](https://martech.org/salesforce-acquires-fin-formerly-known-as-intercom/)), Sierra was valued at **$15.8B** ([Value Add VC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/sierra-ai-valuation-2026-15-8b-series-e-enterprise-ai-agents)), and Decagon at **$4.5B** ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/artificial-intelligence/ai-customer-support-startup-decagon-valued-at-4-5-billion)). That market is consolidating around proprietary models and per-resolution pricing, so it proves demand but offers Grey no opening. Event-triggered knowledge agents, meanwhile, became standard at Glean, Notion, Dust and Copilot Studio between 2025 and 2026 ([Glean](https://docs.glean.com/agents/concepts/content-trigger)). Gartner predicts that 40% of enterprise apps will feature task-specific agents by the end of 2026, up from under 5% in 2025 ([Gartner](https://gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-08-26-gartner-predicts-40-percent-of-enterprise-apps-will-feature-task-specific-ai-agents-by-2026-up-from-less-than-5-percent-in-2025)).

A second tier of proven demand needs little or no generation. Knowledge-gap analytics, which cluster the questions a knowledge base could not answer, are a paid tier everywhere. Kapa's median contract is **$20,700 a year** ([Vendr](https://www.vendr.com/marketplace/kapa-ai)), Intercom gates the feature to an add-on, and Inkeep reserves it for Enterprise ([Intercom](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/11394959-use-ai-powered-content-suggestions-to-improve-fin); [Inkeep](https://agents-docs-7mhm5hg0a.preview.inkeep.com/pricing)). Monitoring and alerts over ingested feeds sell for **$14,400–$19,200 a year at Feedly** ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/202497/Feedly/pricing/)) and $20,000–$40,000 at Klue and Crayon ([Fast.io](https://www.fast.io/resources/best-ai-competitive-intelligence-tools-2026.md)). Theme clustering of customer feedback starts around **$24,000 a year at Unwrap** ([GetApp](https://www.getapp.com.au/software/2082335/unwrap)). Chat has not replaced search either. Algolia's DocSearch v4 puts as-you-type search and "Ask AI" in one box ([Algolia](https://www.algolia.com/blog/product/docsearch-reimagined)), and an NN/g study found people go back to result lists to verify facts, for high-stakes decisions, and to "look at the source directly" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-search-infoseeking/)). Generated artifacts are popular, but the market leader bundles them into a consumer AI subscription ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-notebook/new-study-tools-september-2026/)). Audio Overviews are the only NotebookLM output with official growth data, with daily creation **doubling within two weeks** of the 80-language launch ([Google](https://blog.google/technology/ai/developing-notebooklm/)).

The failures teach as much as the successes. Microsoft **retired Viva Topics and its AI-generated topic pages on 22 February 2025** ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/viva/topics)). Zendesk retired Content Cues, its article-gap suggestions, for **"very low utilization"** ([Zendesk](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/8558652714778)). Nomic pivoted from embedding maps to an agent for architecture, engineering and construction firms ([Nomic](https://docs.nomic.ai/)), Lilac was folded into Databricks ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/databricks-acquires-lilac-to-supercharge-data-quality-efforts-for-gen-ai-apps)), and Microsoft's GraphRAG repository is in maintenance mode ([GitHub](https://github.com/microsoft/graphrag)). Contract-review startup Robin AI went into a distressed sale in late 2025 even though its category was growing ([Legal Technology](https://legaltechnology.com/robin-ai-listed-for-distressed-sale-nine-months-after-making-the-sunday-times-100-tech-list/)). The common thread is that generic views of a corpus struggle: topic pages, embedding maps, and gap reports with no follow-up action. Outputs that land in a workflow with a review step get paid for.

## Every structured use is one typed task aimed at a different target

The patterns above look like a dozen products, but nearly all of them reduce to one object Grey does not yet have: a **task definition**. A task pairs an output contract (a label set, a field schema, a verdict or a document template) with several settings: instructions or a policy; a retrieval scope (a pipeline, a source filter, a graph neighbourhood, or fixed reference sources such as a playbook); a model binding; options for citations, confidence and rationale; an evaluation set; and a version number. The market has converged on this shape from several directions. Databricks exposes `ai_classify` and `ai_extract` as versioned REST endpoints with confidence, rationale and citation options ([Databricks](https://docs.databricks.com/api/ai-functions/v1/ai-extract)). Extend saves extraction configs as versioned "processors" with evaluation suites ([Extend](https://docs.extend.ai/splitting/overview)). AWS calls the same object a blueprint and Box calls it a metadata template ([AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/bda-custom-output-idp.html); [Box](https://developer.box.com/reference/post-ai-extract-structured)). RAG-first vendors are weakest here: Pinecone Assistant offers only a `jsonResponse` flag, with no schema ([Pinecone](https://sdk.pinecone.io/typescript/media/chat.md)). A RAG platform that makes "a typed function grounded in my knowledge base" a first-class object has room to stand out.

### Classification is the simplest task and the clearest gap

The classifier that picks one option from a fixed set is the simplest task. It is also where Grey's knowledge base adds the most that competitors lack. General-purpose functions take labels, label descriptions, instructions and a few examples, and return labels with optional confidence and rationale. **None of the vendors researched returns citations to the knowledge-base passages that justified the label.** Grey can ground a label in two ways. Policy-grounded classification retrieves the relevant section of a written policy or taxonomy and reasons over it. OpenAI's open-weight gpt-oss-safeguard shows this works: the policy is supplied at inference time, so changing it needs no retraining. The model runs on Groq, Ollama and LM Studio ([OpenAI cookbook](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/articles/gpt-oss-safeguard-guide); [Groq](https://groq.com/blog/day-zero-support-for-openai-open-safety-model)), all reachable through Grey's OpenAI-compatible provider kind. Example-grounded classification retrieves the nearest previously labelled items, and it can run on embeddings alone with no generative call.

Large label sets need care. Snowflake warns that more than **20 categories "might reduce classification accuracy"** ([Snowflake](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/sql-reference/functions/ai_classify)). Zendesk caps custom intents at 50 and added sub-categories in November 2025 ([Zendesk](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/8718789695002); [Zendesk](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/9882930657050-Announcing-custom-intent-categories-for-intelligent-triage)). The fix is hierarchical labels, or shortlisting candidate labels by embedding similarity before the model decides. The same technique rescues catalog matching. One practitioner's LLM-only matcher broke down past about 50 products until pgvector shortlisting was added ([SKUai](https://saifsgallery.notion.site/SKUai-321819ee4451803c81e7f5a5431e66b2)).

### The same task runs on one input, a grid, a rule or a schedule

What differs between the commercial products is mostly the target and the trigger, not the task. A task can run on one input passed in by an API, MCP or workflow caller, such as a Forms submission to route or a Flow issue to triage. It can run on one source, such as a contract's fields or a meeting's action items. Run over many sources, it becomes the grid. Rows are sources or graph entities, columns are tasks or free-text questions, and cells carry `{value, reasoning, citations, status}`, with states for AI, edited, verified, flagged and locked, as in Harvey. Bound to a source filter, it becomes a standing rule that fires on each new match, which is how Box Extract, Ragie instructions and Airtable's re-running field agents behave ([Box](https://www.box.com/extract); [Ragie](https://docs.ragie.ai/docs/entity-extraction)). Bound to a clock, it becomes a digest.

Several established products are presets of these combinations. Questionnaire answering is a grid whose rows are questions and whose context includes an approved-answer library. Playbook review is a column with a reference file attached, which Harvey already supports ([Harvey](https://help.harvey.ai/articles/using-review-tables)). A grounding check is a verify task shaped like Google's Check Grounding, which returns a support score and per-claim citations ([Google](https://docs.cloud.google.com/generative-ai-app-builder/docs/check-grounding)).

Grey's knowledge graph also offers a cheap source of contradiction candidates that no competitor reviewed has. When the same entity and relation carry different values across sources (a 30-day return window in one document, 14 days in another), that pair can go to a judge task. Such conflicts are common enough to matter. A 2025 study estimated that **about 3.3% of Wikipedia facts conflict** with other content in the corpus ([arXiv 2509.23233](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.23233)), and Intercom's weekly content suggestions typically include **about 15 contradictions and 5 duplicates** ([Intercom](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/11394959-use-ai-powered-content-suggestions-to-improve-fin)).

### Generated artifacts are tasks with long outputs

An artifact template is a set of sections, each with instructions, its own retrieval scope and a citation requirement. Gong's admin-defined brief types and Writer's playbooks work this way ([Gong](https://help.gong.io/docs/understanding-ai-briefer.md); [Writer](https://support.writer.com/article/298-playbooks)). Artifacts fall into three cost tiers. Text artifacts (briefing documents, FAQs, study guides, show notes, quizzes) need only an LLM. Graph-shaped artifacts (mind maps, timelines, entity pages) are mostly deterministic renders of Grey's entities and relations, with the model writing only labels and summaries. Media artifacts (audio overviews, slides, video) need extra providers. Freshness is the main design problem. Mintlify regenerates documentation pages when a code diff touches them ([Mintlify](https://www.mintlify.com/blog/docs-on-autopilot)), while DeepWiki documents only manual regeneration ([Devin docs](https://docs.devin.ai/work-with-devin/deepwiki)) and draws complaints about stale pages that cannot be removed ([OpenWrt forum](https://forum.openwrt.org/t/important-consideration-deepwiki-ai-generated-docs-may-contain-inaccuracies/249471)). Viva Topics adds a second lesson. Entity pages should be built on demand from the data, with any LLM summary cached and marked as generated, rather than precomputed into a wiki that someone has to curate.

### Automations, workflow steps and write-back deliver the task's output

Automations, workflow steps and write-back are the delivery side of the same object. Workflow builders have converged on six knowledge verbs: retrieve, ask, extract, classify, draft and route. Grey's API today offers only the first two. Event-triggered actions share one shape: a source event, a filter, a grounded step, an action, and a logged run. Glean's content triggers are the most complete design, and Glean recommends at least one filter per trigger because **"unscoped triggers can exhaust hourly quotas quickly"** ([Glean](https://docs.glean.com/agents/concepts/content-trigger)). Dust caps webhook triggers at **42 runs per 24 hours** by default ([Dust](https://docs.dust.tt/docs/triggers)).

Agents increasingly call knowledge servers over MCP, and the protocol's 2026-07-28 revision matters for Grey in two ways. It adds an official tasks extension for long and batch jobs. It also **deprecates Sampling**, telling servers to "integrate directly with LLM provider APIs instead" ([MCP changelog](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/changelog)).

Write-back is shipping everywhere, but always behind human review. SharePoint saves nothing "until you apply the changes" ([Syskit](https://www.syskit.com/blog/knowledge-agent-in-sharepoint)). Guru's feedback-driven auto-unverify is off by default ([Guru](https://help.getguru.com/docs/verifying-and-unverifying-cards)). Zendesk's Knowledge Builder produces drafts labelled "AI-generated" ([Zendesk](https://www.zendesk.com/blog/zendesk-insights/innovation/fuel-your-ai-with-a-knowledge-base-built-in-days-not-months/)).

## BYO makes retrieval-only features nearly free and generative ones a budgeting problem

Grey's rule that every model is customer-attached splits the features cleanly by what each operation calls. One large group needs **no generative model**. It covers faceted search and related sources, duplicate and near-duplicate detection, gap and retrieval-health analytics, saved-query and entity alerts, trend counts, topic clustering, graph analytics, entity pages without summaries, and vector or graph export. These run on embeddings and graph data the customer already paid to create during ingestion, plus at most one query embedding. Commercial products in this group charge heavily for work that, under BYO, costs almost nothing. Unwrap's spike alerts are statistical, with LLM "quality gates" applied only afterwards ([Unwrap](https://docs.unwrap.ai/articles/3984222902-realtime-alerts)). Saved-search alerting is the Elasticsearch percolator pattern, which REA Group uses to match thousands of new listings a day against millions of stored searches ([Elastic](https://community.elastic.co/events/details/elastic-mumbai-presents-elastic-percolation-to-match-new-real-estate-listings-against-saved-searches/)). For Grey, these features cost at most a fraction of a cent of embedding per query, and Grey's full-text search keeps working even when an embedding call fails ([retrieval.js](C:/Pro/geiger-grey/lib/engine/retrieval.js)).

The generative group covers classification, extraction, grids, questionnaires, artifacts, digests and triggered automations. Here BYO helps Grey's economics but adds product requirements. The help is simple: the customer's key pays, so Grey carries no compute risk on recurring or bulk work. OpenAI limited ChatGPT Pulse, its overnight briefing, to the **$200-a-month Pro tier because of compute cost** ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-09-25/chatgpt-feature-offers-users-a-personalized-daily-briefing)), whereas Grey can offer scheduled digests on any plan. The requirements follow from cost now scaling as rows × columns × tokens on someone else's invoice, which customers notice. Harvey advises fewer columns or smaller batches when large tables stall ([Harvey](https://help.harvey.ai/articles/uploading-files-to-harvey)), and Clay users complain that AI-column costs are unpredictable ([Clay community](https://community.clay.com/x/support/c3fhicyuc7n1/concerns-over-gpt-4omini-price-hike-on-claygent-ta)).

Cost control therefore has to be a product feature with four parts. The first is an estimate before every run. The second is a dry run on a handful of rows; Glean, for example, shows the last seven days of matching events before a trigger goes live ([Glean](https://developers.glean.com/guides/triggers/overview)). The third is a cap per job and per automation that pauses rather than overspends. Notion alerts at 80% and 100% of credits ([Notion](https://www.notion.com/product/custom-agents)), and Copilot Studio disables agents at 125% of capacity ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-copilot-studio/requirements-messages-management)). The fourth is a model choice per task or column, so a cheap model fills label columns while a strong one writes analysis. Grey's existing ingestion cost estimate and monthly soft cap are the starting point, but neither covers LLM tasks yet.

BYO also means Grey must own output reliability, because providers enforce schemas unevenly. Vectara reports that `strict` JSON-schema mode is honoured only on OpenAI-family APIs, is dropped for Anthropic and Vertex, and is left to the caller to validate ([Vectara](https://docs.vectara.com/docs/agents/structured-outputs)). Grey must therefore validate every output, repair or retry malformed ones, and treat confidence only as a ranking signal; LlamaExtract warns that its own scores "are not calibrated" ([LlamaIndex](https://developers.llamaindex.ai/python/cloud/llamaextract/features/extensions)). The MCP Sampling deprecation closes the shortcut of letting the calling agent's model do the work, so extraction and classification must run on the project's attached keys. Evaluation needs one more precaution. When a model generates a test set and is then judged on it, its scores inflate through self-enhancement bias ([arXiv 2609.15242](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.15242)). Synthetic eval sets should therefore use different providers for generation and judging when the customer has attached more than one.

Media artifacts are the one family BYO genuinely constrains. Audio overviews need a text-to-speech (TTS) provider. Grey's provider registry has no such capability: it lists only chat, embed, rerank and transcribe ([provider_kinds.js](C:/Pro/geiger-grey/lib/grey/provider_kinds.js)). The providers work but differ. ElevenLabs Text to Dialogue allows any number of speakers but caps each request at 2,000 characters ([ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/capabilities/text-to-dialogue)), while Gemini TTS allows two speakers per request ([Gemini API](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/speech-generation)). The open-source Open Notebook already generates multi-speaker podcasts across 20+ LLM providers and many TTS providers ([GitHub](https://github.com/lfnovo/open-notebook)), so the pattern is feasible. Google's own Podcast API is **no longer allowlisting new customers** ([Google Cloud](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/notebooklm-enterprise/docs/podcast-api)), which leaves a provider-neutral "podcast from sources" endpoint unclaimed. Slides, infographics and video overviews get their look from Google's proprietary image models ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-labs/video-overviews-nano-banana/)), so a BYO version should default to deterministic templates and treat an image provider as optional. Avatar video is out of scope.

Pricing follows from this. Document-processing (IDP) vendors' $5–$50 per 1,000 pages includes the model, so Grey cannot charge per page on that scale while the customer separately pays for tokens. The workable precedent is to charge for orchestration. Relevance AI splits its bill into "Actions" and pass-through "Vendor Credits", and customers who bring their own API keys skip the second meter entirely ([Relevance AI](https://relevanceai.com/docs/admin/subscriptions/new-pricing.md)). Gumloop goes the other way, raising its orchestration fee from 8% to 16% for BYOK users ([Jet Admin](https://www.jetadmin.io/blog/gumloop-pricing/)), which shows BYO can be priced as a premium rather than a discount. Grey already meters this way. It charges for parsed pages and retrievals and records BYOK tokens at zero cost ([billing.js](C:/Pro/geiger-grey/lib/grey/billing.js); [cost.js](C:/Pro/geiger-grey/lib/grey/cost.js)). A new Grey-charged unit per task run or grid cell needs only an extension of the `usage_events.kind` check. Per-resolution pricing, the support-market norm, would look like double-charging on top of the customer's model bill.

| Family | Model calls per operation | BYO fit | What BYO forces Grey to build |
|---|---|---|---|
| Faceted search, related sources, duplicates | One query embedding, or none | Excellent | Nothing beyond the feature itself |
| Gap, coverage and retrieval-health analytics | None; optional LLM write-up | Excellent | On-demand summaries on the customer's key |
| Saved-query, entity and trend alerts | None per new source | Excellent | Thresholds tuned per alert |
| Topic clustering, graph analytics, vector and graph export | None; optional LLM labels | Excellent | Jobs run separately per embedding model |
| Classification | One LLM call per item, or embeddings only | Good | Label validation, eval harness, uncalibrated confidence |
| Extraction, grids, questionnaires | LLM per field or cell (rows × columns) | Good, costly at scale | Estimates, dry runs, hard caps, per-column models |
| Text artifacts and digests | LLM per artifact; map-reduce on long inputs | Good | Long-running jobs, schedule budgets |
| Media derivatives (chapters, show notes) | LLM over the existing transcript | Good | Nothing new; transcription is already BYO |
| Triggered automations and workflow steps | LLM per run | Good with governance | Budgets, rate limits, run logs, key choice per automation |
| Audio overviews | LLM + TTS | Needs a new capability | TTS provider kind, audio-stitching worker |
| Slides, infographics, video | LLM + image model | Weak | Deterministic templates first |
| Head-on support agent | LLM per conversation | Poor commercially | Would compete with leaders on proprietary models |

## Grey already owns most of the parts but lacks four foundations

The codebase is further along than its chat-only surface suggests. `searchChunks` is a self-contained hybrid search that needs no LLM. It runs one vector search per embedding group plus full-text search, adds optional graph expansion, and merges results with reciprocal-rank fusion and optional reranking. It already backs `/retrieve` and the MCP `search` tool ([retrieval.js](C:/Pro/geiger-grey/lib/engine/retrieval.js)). Graph extraction's `extractBatch` is a ready template for any typed task. It uses structured output through the AI SDK, falls back to parsing loose JSON when a provider returns malformed output, sanitises the result, and stops early on fatal errors, all on the project's processing model ([graph_extract.js](C:/Pro/geiger-grey/lib/engine/graph_extract.js); [llm.js](C:/Pro/geiger-grey/lib/engine/llm.js)). The ingestion worker's `graphStage` is a clean hook for further per-source stages. It runs on live chunks after the swap, is gated by a preset flag, records BYOK usage, and degrades to warnings rather than failing the job ([run.mjs](C:/Pro/geiger-grey/worker/ingest/run.mjs)). The evaluation model has the same shape as a grid: sets of cases, and runs of results with a `scores` jsonb, claimed from a lease queue and resumable after a crash ([eval_runner.js](C:/Pro/geiger-grey/lib/engine/eval_runner.js)).

The infrastructure makes new features cheap to add. The worker defines a queue as a small interface (`claim`, `run`, `heartbeat`, `release`) with per-project concurrency, retries and backoff, so a third queue is mostly SQL ([queues.mjs](C:/Pro/geiger-grey/worker/queues.mjs); [worker_queue.sql](C:/Pro/geiger-grey/supabase/migrations/20261018120100_worker_queue.sql)). New endpoints and tools can reuse API-key auth with scopes and rate limits, the `/retrieve` route skeleton, delivery logging, and an MCP tool registry with per-server enable switches ([api_key_auth.js](C:/Pro/geiger-grey/lib/server/api_key_auth.js); [tools.js](C:/Pro/geiger-grey/lib/server/mcp/tools.js)). Human-review primitives exist at two levels. Sources carry an owner, a review date and a verified timestamp. Answers carry thumbs, notes and an "unanswered" flag, which the Feedback screen can promote into eval cases ([ingestion.sql](C:/Pro/geiger-grey/supabase/migrations/20261010120400_ingestion.sql); [feedback.jsx](C:/Pro/geiger-grey/components/internal/screens/quality/feedback.jsx)). The request log already stores each query with the ids, scores and ranks of what was retrieved, which is the raw material for gap analytics ([quality_usage.sql](C:/Pro/geiger-grey/supabase/migrations/20261010120800_quality_usage.sql)).

Four foundations are missing, and every non-chat family needs at least one of them. First, **retrieval cannot be scoped below the knowledge base**. `match_chunks_vector` and `match_chunks_text` accept only a project and a list of knowledge bases, with no source, kind, date or metadata filter ([retrieval.sql](C:/Pro/geiger-grey/supabase/migrations/20261018120200_retrieval.sql)). Per-document extraction, grid cells, "similar to this document" and faceted search all need at least a `p_source_ids` filter. Nor is there a session-authenticated search route for workspace members: every path runs through a published pipeline, an MCP server or the Playground's chat turn. Second, **there is no generic task or job model**. The only job tables are `ingestion_jobs`, tied to one source, and `eval_runs`, tied to one eval set. Third, **nothing stores derived outputs**. There are no tables for labels, extracted fields or artifacts, and no review state for any new output. A design constraint applies here because chunk ids are not stable. `swap_source_chunks` hard-deletes a source's live chunks whenever it re-chunks, so labels and citations must anchor on the source id plus an ordinal, page or timestamp, and human corrections must live outside the chunks table. Fourth, **there is no outbound channel of any kind**: no webhooks, no scheduler, no email sender, and no code that writes notifications. `alert_emails` and `retention_days` are stored, but nothing acts on them, and the spec's `alert` cap behaviour "proceeds (sending alerts out of scope)" ([backend spec](C:/Pro/geiger-grey/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-17-grey-backend-design.md)).

Smaller gaps sit behind those four. Today's schemas are static zod objects in code, so user-defined tasks need a bridge from JSON Schema, and whether the pinned AI SDK v7 converts JSON Schema directly was not verified. Check constraints on `ingestion_jobs.stage`, `usage_events.kind`, `request_logs.channel` and `conversations.channel` mean every new stage, billing unit or channel needs a migration. The prompt builder caps context at 6,000 tokens and 1,200 per chunk ([prompt.js](C:/Pro/geiger-grey/lib/grey/prompt.js)), so whole-document tasks need map-reduce over chunks or the cached full-text parse that ingestion already keeps. On the delivery side, the OpenAI-compatible endpoint rejects `tools` and ignores `response_format` ([openai_frames.js](C:/Pro/geiger-grey/lib/grey/openai_frames.js)), API keys know only the scopes `retrieve`, `chat` and `mcp` ([api-keys route](C:/Pro/geiger-grey/app/api/api-keys/route.js)), and routes time out at 60 and 300 seconds, so anything bigger than a single item needs a submit-and-poll design.

The largest dependency is operational rather than code: **the worker host is still undecided** (spec decision D3). Every queued feature (grids, batch runs, on-ingest tasks, schedules) waits on that decision. Synchronous single-item tasks and LLM-free features do not.

The rest of the suite removes much of the outbound work. Geiger Forms already ships **signed HMAC webhooks with up to eight delivery attempts, replay, Slack posts and email** ([webhooks.js](C:/Pro/geiger-forms/lib/server/webhooks.js)). It also has an automations engine that runs condition-and-action lists on every submission ([side-effects.js](C:/Pro/geiger-forms/lib/server/side-effects.js)). Forms already writes into Flow, creating `flow.issues` rows through the service role ([flow.js](C:/Pro/geiger-forms/lib/server/flow.js)), which is a working precedent for Grey turning a meeting's action items into Flow issues. Grey already depends on `@geiger/notify` but uses it only to display the bell. The package also ships `createServerNotifier`, which lets a service-role caller send into the suite-wide bell ([geiger-notify README](C:/Pro/geiger-notify/README.md)). Flow's project custom fields (text, number, select, date, boolean and formula) are natural targets for classification and extraction results ([custom_fields.sql](C:/Pro/geiger-flow/supabase/migrations/20260914155203_custom_fields.sql)). Grey can copy its outbound layer from its siblings rather than invent it, and the backend spec already plans for Forms to call Grey from `runSubmissionEffects` ([backend spec](C:/Pro/geiger-grey/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-17-grey-backend-design.md)).

## Ship in five steps, starting with what needs no model

The order below weighs four things: commercial proof, cost under BYO, how much existing code each step reuses, and what each step unlocks for the next. It deliberately puts the most proven surface, the grid, third rather than first. The grid needs the filters from step one and the task runner from step two, and its batch runs need the worker host decided.

**Step 1: scoped retrieval, search, gap analytics and suite notifications.** Add source, kind, origin, date and entity filters to the match functions and to `searchChunks`. Then build a member-facing Search screen with a Search/Ask toggle, results grouped by source, and facets, including an entity facet that most search vendors cannot offer because they have no graph underneath. The same screen gets a "related sources" panel, and uploads get a near-duplicate warning. Next, build a Gaps view from the request log and `unanswered` flags, using low top-score retrievals as a "no good match" signal that needs no LLM. Each gap gets an owner, a status and an "add a source and re-test" action, because Zendesk's retirement of Content Cues shows that a report with no way to act on it goes unused. Finally, send Grey's existing attention items (failed sources, provider errors, review dates, a reached spending cap) into the suite bell through `createServerNotifier`. None of this calls a generative model or needs the worker, and the filters are a prerequisite for steps two and three.

**Step 2: the task primitive, with classify and extract.** Add versioned task definitions, modelled on pipeline versions. Each holds the schema, instructions, retrieval binding and model binding, defaulting to the project's processing model. The runner reuses the `extractBatch` pattern with validation, repair and retry, and returns citations anchored to source and position, a rationale, and confidence labelled as uncalibrated. Classification ships with two modes: an embeddings-only mode for near-zero-cost routing, and an LLM mode that grounds labels in a policy or in past examples and cites its evidence, which is the gap no major vendor fills. Expose each task as `POST /api/v1/tasks/{id}/run` under a new key scope, as an MCP tool on any server that enables it, and as a test bench in the Playground. Reuse eval sets as the accuracy harness, so admins can measure a classifier before turning it on, much as Intercom offers a preview tool before Fin Attributes go live. Single-item tasks finish within route time limits, so this step does not wait for the worker host. It is also the step that turns Grey from a place to ask questions into a component other apps call.

**Step 3: the grid.** Rows are a source filter or a set of graph entities. Columns are tasks or free-text questions. Cells hold a value, reasoning, citations and a review status, and human edits win. Every run shows a cost estimate before it starts, offers a dry run on a few rows, respects a hard cap and allows a model choice per column, and single cells can be refreshed on their own. Results export to CSV and XLSX. The eval runner's set, run and result structure is the template for the tables and the resumable queue. Entity rows are the differentiator. Each one aggregates every source that mentions a company or person, as Hebbia's company rows do, which is something document-processing vendors that handle one file at a time cannot offer.

**Step 4: standing rules, alerts and write-back.** Let any task or grid column become a standing rule that runs on every new source matching a filter. A rule writes labels and fields onto the source and typed properties onto graph entities. Every rule output carries a review state, and rules whose results trigger an action can require approval first. The outbound layer then needs three channels. Signed webhooks for source and task events can be copied from the Forms delivery code. Saved-search and entity-watch alerts are checked after each chunk swap by comparing each saved query with the new chunks' embeddings, which ingestion has already computed. A schedule table, claimed by the worker, drives digests. Then connect the suite in three places: meeting recordings yield action items that become Flow issues, classification results fill Flow custom fields, and Forms automations gain a "Grey classify" or "Grey extract" action. Every automation gets a budget, a rate limit, a run log, an owner and an explicit choice of provider key. MCP gains write tools (add a source, propose a fact), and whatever they add stays pending review, and out of retrieval, until approved.

**Step 5: generated artifacts and questionnaire answering.** This step adds artifact templates (briefing document, FAQ, study guide), plus chapters and show notes generated automatically for media on ingest. Entity pages are built on demand from the graph, and a scheduled "what changed" digest is built from source and graph timestamps, with the model writing only the narrative. Each artifact records the sources it used, so it can be marked stale when they change. Questionnaire answering arrives as a grid preset, with question rows, an approved-answer library ranked above raw chunks, and export back to the original format.

| Step | What ships | Model cost under BYO | Needs the worker host |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Scoped retrieval, Search screen, related sources, duplicate check, Gaps view, suite notifications | None, or one embedding per query | No |
| 2 | Task definitions; classify and extract over REST, MCP and the Playground | One call per item; classify also has an embeddings-only mode | No |
| 3 | Grid over sources or entities, with estimates, caps and exports | Rows × columns, capped | Yes |
| 4 | Standing rules, webhooks, alerts, schedules, Flow and Forms write-back, MCP write tools | Per run, budgeted per automation | Yes |
| 5 | Artifact templates, media derivatives, entity pages, digests, questionnaires | Per artifact or per question | Yes |

Two alternative orderings deserve a fair hearing. The first would lead with generated artifacts, since Gemini Notebook has taught users to expect one-click briefs and audio, and they demo well. But Google bundles them into a consumer subscription, and the buyers this research found paying are paying for workflow outcomes. Artifacts built after the task runner are also cheaper to make, because each becomes another template rather than a separate engine. The second would lead with the grid because its proof is strongest. That is defensible, but a grid shipped without scoped retrieval, a task runner and a production worker would only be a demo. Placing it third keeps it in the first wave while letting it stand on finished foundations.

One caveat applies to the evidence itself. The strongest grid and questionnaire proof comes from enterprise legal, finance and security buyers, while Grey's likely buyers are agencies and SMB teams. Horizontal AI-column products suggest the pattern works at lower prices, but nothing gathered measures demand in Grey's own segments. After the five steps, the next candidates, roughly in order, are audio overviews (once a TTS provider kind and an audio-stitching worker exist), contradiction detection from conflicting graph relations together with a grounding-check endpoint that also hardens chat, entity matching against a catalog, topic clustering with trend alerts, vector and graph export, and synthetic eval-set generation. Several things should stay off the roadmap: a standalone embedding-map product, a precomputed wiki of every entity, a head-on support agent sold per resolution, avatar video, and slides that depend on a proprietary image model.

## Conclusion

Under BYO, the usual SaaS economics invert. Features that call no generative model are where Grey can charge while paying nothing, because search, gap analytics, alerts and graph views run on data the customer has already paid to create. Features that do call a model are where Grey earns trust rather than margin. That makes the cost estimate, the dry run and the hard cap product features in their own right rather than settings. It also means the billable unit should be Grey's orchestration (a task run, a grid cell), never anything that looks like resold tokens.

The research also changes what makes Grey different outside chat. It is not better answers. Grey can offer three things that competitors each lack at least one of. The first is citations on every structured output, which no major classifier returns. The second is a knowledge graph that yields entity rows, contradiction candidates and deterministic timelines. The third is sibling apps (Forms automations, Flow issues and fields, the suite bell) where a task's result becomes an action without any connector. The typed task is what links the three, which is why it, rather than another chat surface, is the thing to build next. And the decision that gates most of that roadmap is an operational one, not a product one: choosing where the worker runs.
